# Changes made since Part II
Since completing Part II, I made several key adjustments to enhance both the clarity and aesthetics of the website. First, I replaced all unclear images, including switching a static map screenshot to an animated GIF, which more effectively shows the increasing stringency of legal requirements over time. Additionally, I updated the map at last with a clearer template, which aligns better with the unified tone of the website.

In terms of design, I adopted a consistent brown color scheme throughout the website, as it’s more visually appealing compared to a black-and-white palette. This change helped create a more cohesive and engaging look. I also expanded the call-to-action section by adding more practical suggestions tailored to the target audience. For the gender pay gap chart, I improved the clarity of the axes, making the data easier to interpret.

Lastly, I added sources for all the images and ensured all data sources were properly cited at the end of the page. These changes contribute to a more professional final product.

## The audience
Initially, my goal was to reach a broad audience, including anyone who wanted to advocate for women. However, based on feedback from Part II, I realized that this approach was too general. In order to have a greater impact, I refined my target audience to include business leaders and policymakers - those who have the power and resources to implement real, systemic change. By narrowing the audience, I wanted to target those who can influence corporate policy and legislation, ensuring that the proposed solutions translate into actionable steps. Such a focus allows the content to be more targeted and more effective in driving change in the areas that matter most.

Based on my refined target audience of business leaders and policymakers, I’ve also adjusted my call to action to be more direct and results-driven. Instead of broadly encouraging support for gender equality, I now focus on urging these decision-makers to implement corporate policies that drive equality. I also emphasize the importance of advocating for legislative reforms that enforce equal pay laws and promote women’s workplace rights. By making the call more specific and aligned with their ability to enact change, I hope to ensure that my solutions are not just discussed, but actually put into action.

## Final design decisions and Final thoughts
Along the way, I made several design decisions that were key to refining the website for a clearer, more engaging experience. One significant learning moment came from balancing aesthetics with functionality. For example, while I initially aimed for a black-and-white palette, I realized through feedback that a warmer brown color scheme was more visually appealing and better unified the page’s tone. This taught me the importance of color in setting the right mood for the audience.

Additionally, I had to carefully consider the readability of the data visualizations. My project originally included a vast amount of data, as I wanted to cover as many aspects as possible. However, I realized that this approach overwhelmed the audience, making it hard for them to focus on the most critical insights. The charts became cluttered, diluting the key messages I wanted to convey. To address this, I decided to narrow my focus to workplace-related gender disparities while still touching on rights issues with striking data. I also simplified the data by using only the most recent years in certain visualizations. This helped streamline the story, making it more readable and impactful for the audience. By focusing on fewer, stronger data points, I ensured the story had greater clarity and emotional resonance.

Furthermore, I placed too much emphasis on data at first, aiming to include as many different data points as possible to convey more information. This led to an overload of visuals, with various datasets piling up and making it harder for the audience to focus on the key takeaways. Working through these design tasks, I realized that the visual design should support the narrative rather than overshadow it. Every decision—from color choices to which data to present and how to interact with it—needed to enhance the story's clarity and impact. By simplifying the data and focusing on emotional resonance, I was able to create a more compelling and cohesive final product that resonates more effectively with the audience.

In the "Is it only about economics" section, I strategically used white space as a powerful tool to allow the audience a moment of reflection. By minimizing visual clutter in this part of the webpage, I created a pause, giving viewers time to absorb the gravity of the message. This intentional design choice also made the data and insights in that section more impactful, leading to a more dramatic and thought-provoking result. The white space helped highlight the significance of gender inequality beyond economics, reinforcing the emotional resonance of the content.
